## Technique Execution Results

### Phase 1 — Question Storming: Key Discoveries

**Customer & Problem Space:**

- **[Customer #1]: The Trapped AI Operator** — White-collar workers and startup builders who have committed to AI-assisted workflows but spend significant time in prompt iteration loops, manually sequencing AI steps, and correcting outputs. They are AI believers stuck in the adoption trough between "AI is fast" and "AI is autonomous."

- **[Problem #2]: The Forgotten Technique Problem** — Operators solve hard AI-direction problems once, never document them, then solve them again from scratch next time. SKILLS.md encodes expert reasoning patterns so the technique outlives the session — turning one-time effort into reusable operator leverage.

**Market & Competitive Space:**

- **[Market #3]: The 90% Quality Threshold** — Clients don't buy perfection; they buy "good enough to serve their goals." An AI operator delivering 90% of expert quality at speed is a better product than a human expert billing hourly. The value bar is client outcomes, not craft.

- **[Market #4]: Human-in-the-Loop Is Structural, Not a Bug** — The internet was built for humans: authentication, crawling, data integration all require human judgment at boundary points. Existing tools fail by trying to eliminate the human entirely. The winning approach embraces it — make human integration minimal, high-leverage, and guided by SKILLS.md.

**Business Architecture:**

- **[Business Model #5]: The AI Execution Squad Marketplace** — The product is a service layer connecting clients with dedicated AI execution squads — small teams of human AI operators sharing domain SKILLS.md expertise. Value flows: Client pays → Squad delivers 90% expert quality with internal peer review → SKILLS.md handles expertise encoding. Quality is ensured by the squad, not a single operator. Closer to a specialized agency model than a freelancer marketplace.

- **[Moat #6]: The Access Paradox** — SKILLS.md's GitHub/CLI distribution accidentally creates a barrier. It protects skills from non-technical competitors but locks out non-technical operators who could multiply the platform's reach. The real moat should come from squad iteration quality and squad reputation, not CLI friction.

- **[Risk #7]: The Autonomy Extinction Bet** — The entire business bets that human-in-the-loop remains structurally necessary. If AGI collapses this window, the business collapses. The window is estimated at 3–7 years based on infrastructure inertia. Strategy must maximize value capture within the window by building squad reputation and SKILLS.md depth that are hard to replicate.

**MVP & Go-to-Market:**

- **[MVP #8]: Government Digitization as Sole MVP Vertical** — MVP scope is narrowed to government document digitization and translation only. Content marketing is explicitly out of MVP scope — it is a future phase targeting the broad commercial market. This removes the need for content marketing expert operators at launch and concentrates all SKILLS.md iteration on a single, well-defined task type.

- **[Moat #9]: The Iteration Flywheel** — Squads that leave take a snapshot of SKILLS.md but lose the living improvement stream. A SKILLS.md iterated across 500 squad-delivered tasks is categorically better than one used on 5. The moat is velocity of improvement compounded by squad peer review, not IP ownership.

- **[Brand #10]: Squads as Reputation Nodes** — The platform's brand identity lives in its named AI execution squads, not individuals or technology. A squad with a verified track record of SKILLS.md-powered deliverables becomes a durable trust signal — squad reputation outlives any individual operator exit. Government clients, in particular, prefer contracting with a team over a single contractor.

**MVP Refinements:**

- **[Strategy #11]: The Human Veil Pricing Model** — The platform presents as a human-operated content agency externally. AI involvement is internal infrastructure. Clients pay for human-reputation-backed delivery at agency pricing. SKILLS.md quality justifies cost relative to pure AI commodity pricing.

- **[Strategy #12]: The Decades Bet** — The platform's longevity thesis is that human-centric computer infrastructure (auth, crawling, sessions, UI) takes decades to redesign for AI — not years. The window is long enough to build a defensible network.

- **[Strategy #13]: The Complexity Ladder** — MVP deliberately targets low-complexity, high-volume work (document digitization, translation) where speed and volume matter more than creativity. Revenue and SKILLS.md iteration from this volume fund the climb up the complexity ladder.

- **[Strategy #14]: Government as Anchor Client + Moat** — The MVP is pitched to a city government as an exclusive program. This provides: protected client relationship, predictable high-volume work to iterate SKILLS.md, government reputation as trust signal, and exclusive contract as competitive barrier. Government contact already warm and promising. The city IS the moat.

- **[MVP #15]: Document Digitization + Translation as True Beachhead** — Government's most immediate and voluminous pain is converting paper records to digital and translating documents. Zero creativity required — just accuracy, speed, and volume. Binary quality check. Perfect first squad SKILLS.md: well-defined inputs, internal peer review catches errors before delivery, massive government backlog.

#### PHASE 1: CONCIERGE MVP (Weeks 1–6)

**Hypothesis:** A small AI execution squad (2–3 people) can deliver document digitization and/or translation at acceptable accuracy and speed — validating that government will accept and pay for squad-delivered output.

**Experiment:**
- Assemble a founding squad of 2–3 people (can include yourself)
- Take 1 small real batch of government documents (paper-to-digital or translation)
- Deliver with internal peer review — 1 person does, 1 person checks
- Measure: accuracy %, turnaround time, client reaction

**Success signals:**
- Client confirms output is "good enough"
- Client willing to pay or sign a pilot agreement
- Internal peer review catches errors before delivery (validates squad model)

**Failure signals → Pivots:**
- Quality too low → iterate SKILLS.md before next batch, or narrow scope further
- "We don't actually need this outsourced" → wrong work type, pivot to translation-only or different document category

---

#### PHASE 2: BUILD SKILLS.MD + VALIDATE SQUAD AT SCALE (Weeks 4–16)

**Hypothesis:** SKILLS.md + squad peer review can standardize digitization/translation to ≥90% accuracy, with the squad handling 5× more volume vs. manual with individual oversight.

**Experiment:** Run 50–100 real jobs through the squad using SKILLS.md

**Metrics to track:**
- Accuracy rate per job (target: ≥90%) — squad peer review as quality gate
- Squad throughput per week (target: 5× Concierge MVP baseline)
- Client rejection rate (target: ≤10%)
- Revenue per squad-hour
- Error catch rate in peer review (validates squad quality model)

**Success signals:** All metrics above threshold → proceed to anchor contract pitch

**Failure signals → Pivots:**
- Accuracy plateaus below 85% even with peer review → SKILLS.md needs domain expert rewrite
- Peer review catches >50% of outputs → squad needs training, not just better SKILLS.md
- Max 3 iteration sprints (2 weeks each) before pivoting work type

---

#### PHASE 3: ANCHOR CONTRACT (Weeks 10–20)

**Hypothesis:** Government will sign a recurring or exclusive contract, not just accept one pilot batch.

**Experiment:** Pitch full contract using pilot results as proof — show accuracy %, turnaround, cost vs. current process

**Milestone:** Signed contract (volume + rate agreed)
**Protection:** Seek exclusivity clause if possible

**Failure signal → Pivot:** If gov only wants ad-hoc → pivot to commercial clients using gov pilot as social proof

---

#### PHASE 4: EXPAND WITHIN GOVERNMENT (Months 5–9)

**PMF Signal Checklist:**
- [ ] Client asking for additional work types (data entry, records management, form processing)?
- [ ] Client referring platform to other departments?
- [ ] Revenue predictable month-over-month?

**If ≥2 signals YES → PMF within gov vertical achieved**

**Expand to:** Additional government digitization work types (same client, same complexity tier)
- Example expansions: form digitization, records indexing, bilingual document processing
- Only add new SKILLS.md after current one hits ≥90% accuracy baseline
- Squad reputation compounds with each successful work type added
- Content marketing deliberately excluded from this phase

---

#### PHASE 5: COMMERCIAL BEACHHEAD — CONTENT MARKETING (Months 12–24)

With a verified government track record, pivot to the broad commercial market using content marketing as the entry point for startups, SMEs, and consumer brands.

**Why content marketing here, not earlier:**
- Requires creative judgment (higher SKILLS.md complexity than digitization)
- Requires a different squad profile (content specialists, not digitization specialists)
- Needs a reputation signal to sell — gov squad track record IS that signal

**Growth engine:** Squad reputation as network signal (portfolio, case studies, client referrals)

**Scale gates (only expand when):**
- Founding squad at ≥80% capacity consistently
- Government digitization revenue is stable and self-sustaining
- Content marketing SKILLS.md iterated ≥50 real tasks by a dedicated content squad before adding to service menu

---

#### PHASE 6: SCALE (Month 18+)

**Scale ONLY when all 4 conditions are true:**
- [ ] Revenue per squad is predictable and growing
- [ ] SKILLS.md iteration velocity is compounding (each version measurably better)
- [ ] Squad stability >80% member retention annually
- [ ] Client referral rate >30% of new business

**Scale levers (in order):**
1. Grow existing squads (same domains, more volume per squad)
2. Form new specialized squads (new domains, new SKILLS.md)
3. Geographic expansion (other cities/provinces with dedicated squads)
4. Platform layer (let squads self-register with verified track records + SKILLS.md marketplace)
